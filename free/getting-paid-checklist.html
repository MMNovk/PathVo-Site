The Getting-Paid Checklist

Before you say yes

  • Budget range said out loud by both sides — never "we haven't set one."
  • Deadline has a stated reason, or it's treated as soft.
  • Decision-maker named. Every unnamed stakeholder = a future revision round.
  • Payment terms stated in one sentence: "Invoices are due [N] days from invoice date."
  • Deposit for new clients ([25–50]%) — commitment is information.

Full playbook: scoping a project so it doesn't eat your month

Day one

  • Kickoff email sent: scope + not-list, timeline with "end date moves with feedback," revision count, named approver, payment terms restated.
  • "Tell me today if any of this doesn't match — while it's cheap."

Template: the kickoff email that prevents bad projects

The moment work ends

  • Invoice sent within 24 hours of final delivery — every day late adds a day to their clock.
  • Reminder dates put in your calendar the day you invoice: +1, +7, +21 days after due.
  • If the client says something spontaneously nice: that's the peak — ask for the testimonial or referral now.

Systems: the 10-minute invoicing fix · asking without cringing

If the invoice goes overdue

  • Day 1–2: friendly nudge — assume it sank; offer to fix any invoice issue.
  • Day 7–10: firm — restate amount and date; ask for a payment date, not payment.
  • Day 21+: final — pause work, name the late fee, name the next step with a date.
  • Never: apologies, emotion, or threats you won't carry out.

Exact emails: the 3-stage reminder system

Twice a year

  • Rate review: raise across clients at once, 30–60 days notice, no apology.
  • Oldest-cheapest client gets the bigger jump and the warmer conversation.
